How does it work?

Each life is an identical three-arm pendulum released from almost the same point. Only the ball at its tip can meet a passing ring. Rings come and go. Their paths may be chaotic, but the physics follows the same rules for everyone.

When a pendulum reaches practical rest, that life's journey ends. Some reach rest sooner than others. The game never chooses a winner.

The world behind their stories

  • Same beginnings. Every life has the same three-arm pendulum. The initial gap is a thousandth of a degree.
  • A different world every new game. Encounters and their positions change with the game's seed. Replaying the same game restores the exact world.
  • Opportunity has a place and time. Rings are temporary. The ball at the tip must pass through a ring to record an encounter.
  • Chances follow the movement. Most encounters are below the pivot, where the pendulums move more often. Encounters above it are rare.
  • Nothing is guaranteed. Two lives can pass near the same opportunity and experience different things. Encountering a ring does not change the pendulum's physics.
  • Quiet years have a purpose. Once the last opportunities have passed, the final movements can accelerate automatically, up to a 1,000× target, until practical rest.
  • Every story has an ending. The pendulums come to rest in their own order. You can rewind the journey to see when their paths divided.
More about the physics

The pendulums move in the same physical plane, drawn at small depth offsets so their paths can be seen. All three arms start almost aligned, at an angle with a slight seeded jitter. The same seed and settings replay the same motion. A numerical threshold defines practical rest because damped motion approaches exact rest gradually.
